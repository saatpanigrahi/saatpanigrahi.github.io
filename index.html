About

I am a predoctoral research fellow at Stanford University. I hold a Master's degree in Econometrics and Mathematical Economics from the London School of Economics and a Bachelor's degree in Economics and Mathematics from New York University.

Beyond academia, I worked at TollBit, an early-stage, premier VC-backed content marketplace building the key infrastructure that lets AI companies license content from publishers. There, I worked on industry-setting, in-depth data research reports and helped build the foundations of the company's new data product.

Research

Working Papers

Missing Middle Housing

Abstract

Deregulating middle housing has been cited as crucial for alleviating urban housing supply constraints. In this context, I examine Minneapolis Ordinance 2019–00217, enacted January 1, 2020, which abolished single-family zoning citywide and authorized duplex and triplex construction. Leveraging variation in permit issuance between treated single-family tracts in Minneapolis and comparable tracts in surrounding jurisdictions within the Minneapolis–St. Paul–Bloomington Metropolitan Statistical Area (MSA), I estimate the reform’s impact on housing construction using a partially pooled synthetic control estimator. I find a substantial initial decline in single-family permit issuance immediately following the reform, which attenuates in subsequent years—likely attributable to ongoing legal uncertainty associated with the 2040 Plan. Conversely, I detect only modest increases in duplex and triplex construction. Heterogeneity analysis further reveals that reductions in single-family permits are primarily concentrated in economically disadvantaged neighborhoods. Additionally, I document that restrictive Floor Area Ratio (FAR) policies induced bunching at permissible limits and led to observable shifts in the sizes of newly constructed duplexes and triplexes post-reform. Finally, I find a statistically significant short-term increase in single-family home prices during the initial months following announcement of the policy.
